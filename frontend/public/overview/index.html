Leeway EV trip planner · mainland US · product overview

Every EV planner finds you a charger

This one also takes a detour to dodge a left turn

Leeway plans road trips from the range your car shows at 100% today, not the factory figure, and tells you what will be left in the battery when you arrive. Then it spends a detour budget you set, in minutes, on avoiding the left turn with no signal, the unsignaled crossing of a four-lane road and the rail crossing. In California it also routes around live Caltrans lane closures.

The problem

Your car is not the car on the spec sheet

A 2021 Model 3 Standard Range Plus was rated at 263 miles new. The one this was built for now shows 205. Batteries lose capacity with age, and no two lose it at the same rate, so a planner working from the factory figure can hand you a charging stop you won't reach.

Leeway asks what your car does at 100% now and answers with how much room you will have on arrival. That margin is the name.

Safety

How the avoidance works

Four hazards, each with its own switch: left turns across big roads, unsignaled crossings of roads with four or more lanes, rail crossings, and active Caltrans construction in California. Beside them is a detour budget: flag only, or +5, +10 or +20 minutes. At +5, Leeway routes around any flagged spot it can avoid for five minutes or less and warns about the rest. +5 is the default, so someone who never opens the options is still steered around the cheap ones.

Signals, lane counts and rail crossings come from OpenStreetMap; closures from the Caltrans feed; turn geometry is measured off the route line itself. Steep descents, twisty sections, sun glare and strong wind get a warning and no reroute. When the OpenStreetMap servers are slow, a hazard check gives up rather than hold up the plan, so a flag can go missing.

Settings

The tabs are for when you disagree

The defaults are already the cautious answer, so a good plan needs nothing in here touched. Each control says what it does and what it costs. A collapsed tab shows a dot when something inside differs from the default, so a changed setting can't hide.

Helper text under the reserve floor

Finding a stop

Ask for a stop in a sentence

Type or speak into the bar above the map: "Add an In-N-Out stop after one hour of driving." Gemini turns the sentence into a search, including the budget buried in it: "won't add much time" means five minutes, "I don't mind a longer detour" means twenty. Each result is priced by routing to the door and on to your destination, minus the drive you were making anyway, and the list is ordered by that cost. If nothing fits the budget it says so instead of widening it.

On the road, and after

A card for the drive, and a question afterwards

The trip card cuts the plan to what you need behind the wheel: leave with 68%, the Getty at 3 miles, the Supercharger at 60 miles where you arrive at 25% and charge to 80%, then 28 miles to arrive at 62%. It fits on one screen so it can be screenshotted, because the car's own screen can't open a web app. Any stop can also go out as a maps link, which the Tesla app accepts.

Next time you open Leeway it asks how the trip went and sets what happened beside what it predicted. After three or more trips that each used at least 10% of the battery, it starts correcting its estimates toward your car, weighting recent trips more. The correction is lopsided on purpose: a car that used more than predicted gets the full correction, one that used less gets half, and plans never get more than 10% bolder than the stock estimate. When a correction is in use, the plan says so.

Sample trips on a first visit are labeled "try:" so nobody mistakes them for their own history. When the routing quota runs out, the verdict says planning couldn't run instead of echoing your starting battery back as an answer.

Smaller things

The rest of it

Real range
Don't know your 100% range? Copy the battery % and the range shown off the car's screen and Leeway does the arithmetic. Each reading is dated, so the list doubles as a record of the pack's decline.
Alternatives
"See other routes" compares whole corridors (I-5 against the 101 against the 99) and replans along the one you pick. Skip a charger and the plan is rebuilt around the rest.
Your own stops
Click the map to drop a waypoint; drag any point to reorder. Three stops at most.
Departure time
Weather becomes that hour's forecast, sun glare is computed for then, and closures are filtered to what will still be active. A break rhythm ("at least every 2h35") plans stops for the driver too.
Addresses
When the main geocoder only finds the street, the US Census geocoder places the house number.
Accuracy record
Every logged trip, predicted against actual, with no favorable rounding.
Phone
Installs as a web app from the browser. A native iOS build in a Capacitor shell adds a reminder to log the trip once you should have arrived, and the system share sheet. No offline claim: every plan needs live routing, charging and weather data.
Privacy
No account, analytics or advertising. Range, preferences and trip history stay on your device.

Built on

Where the numbers come from

Free, on free infrastructure, covering the mainland US.

Routing
OpenRouteService: geometry, duration, and the elevation profile the range math walks.
Chargers
Open Charge Map: network, connectors, stall counts, power and cost.
Weather
Open-Meteo: live conditions, or the hourly forecast up to 7 days out.
Hazards
OpenStreetMap via Overpass: signals, lane counts and rail crossings.
Closures
Caltrans Lane Closure System. California only; no other state publishes a free equivalent, so the switch does nothing elsewhere.
Stop search
Gemini, to turn a sentence into a search.
Built with
React, TypeScript and MapLibre GL on GitHub Pages; FastAPI on Render; Capacitor for iOS.
